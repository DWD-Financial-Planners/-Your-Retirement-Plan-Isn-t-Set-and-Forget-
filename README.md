## 🧠 Introduction: Why “Set and Forget” No Longer Works

There was a time when creating a retirement plan felt like a once-off task.  
You met with a financial advisor, selected your investments, and left everything to run in the background.

Today, that approach can quietly cost you.

Financial markets shift. Inflation rises. Careers evolve. Life changes—often unexpectedly.  
If your retirement plan isn’t evolving with you, it’s slowly becoming outdated.

At **DWD Financial Planners**, we believe your financial strategy should be **dynamic, responsive, and aligned with your life at every stage**.

---

## ⚠️ The Hidden Risk of Standing Still

A plan that isn’t reviewed regularly can fall out of sync with your reality.

✔ Your expenses may increase without you adjusting your savings  
✔ Your income may change, but your contributions stay the same  
✔ Your investments may underperform without you noticing  
✔ Your risk level may no longer match your life stage  

> The biggest mistake isn’t making a bad decision—it’s making no decision at all.

---

## 🔄 Why Reviewing Your Retirement Plan Changes Everything

Think of your retirement plan as a **living strategy**, not a fixed document.

When you review it consistently, you gain control and clarity over your financial future.

✔ You identify underperforming investments early  
✔ You realign your goals with your current lifestyle  
✔ You optimise your tax strategy for better efficiency  
✔ You adjust your risk as you move closer to retirement  

Consistent reviews don’t just protect your plan—they strengthen it.

---

## 📅 When Should You Review Your Plan?

The most effective strategy is simple and powerful:

✔ Review your retirement plan **once every year**  
✔ Review it **immediately after major life changes**

---

### 🔑 Life Events That Should Trigger a Review

✔ Starting a new job or changing careers  
✔ Receiving a salary increase or bonus  
✔ Getting married or divorced  
✔ Having children  
✔ Buying or selling property  
✔ Receiving an inheritance  
✔ Moving closer to retirement  

Each of these moments impacts your financial future—and your plan should reflect it.
